The complete cost equation behind every screen that shows a cost figure.

The Equation

Spot(t)  = max( Required(t) − B , 0 )
Idle(t)   = max( B − Required(t) , 0 )
NormalIdle(t) = min( Idle(t) , FreeAllowance )
PenalIdle(t)  = max( Idle(t) − FreeAllowance , 0 )
Shortfall(t) = max( Spot(t) − SpotCap , 0 )
UnmetTonnes(t) = Shortfall(t) × RakeCapacity

Total(B) = N×B×BaseCost  Base Fleet Cost
     + Σ Spot(t)×SpotCost  Spot Cost
     + Σ NormalIdle(t)×DemurrageCost  Demurrage
     + Σ PenalIdle(t)×PunitiveDemurrage  Demurrage Penalty
     + Σ UnmetTonnes(t)×ShortageCost  Shortage Penalty

Demurrage is charged on idle base-fleet capacity -- an overcommitted captive/leased fleet sitting unused on a low-demand day -- mirroring the project proposal's own framing ("demurrage / overbooking charges during slumps"), rather than a generic siding-delay fee. This is a modeling choice, stated here rather than presented as an unquestioned fact. Min-service-level and the spot-availability cap are soft targets reported alongside the cost-optimal point, not hard constraints baked into the objective.

Demurrage is charged in two tiers, mirroring the free-time-then-penal structure of real Indian Railways demurrage: idle capacity up to the Free Allowance is charged the normal rate, and everything above it the higher Punitive Demurrage rate. This makes over-committing the base fleet progressively more expensive rather than linearly so. Keeping the punitive rate at or above the normal rate is what keeps Total(B) convex -- see A5; a lower punitive rate is permitted (the search stays exact) but forfeits that property.
