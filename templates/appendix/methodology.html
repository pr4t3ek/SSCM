Why Breakpoint Search, Not a General Solver

Total(B) is a finite sum of max(linear, 0) hinge terms plus a linear term in B, so it is piecewise-linear. A piecewise-linear function's minimum over a bounded interval is always attained at one of its breakpoints or an endpoint -- here the sorted union, across every day, of Required(t), Required(t) − SpotCap, and Required(t) + FreeAllowance (the last from the two-tier demurrage charge). Enumerating those breakpoints and evaluating cost at each is exact (not an approximation like a grid search), and fast: O(N) candidate points × O(N) vectorized cost evaluation for N ≈ 300–365 days -- well under the latency budget needed for the What-If Simulator to recompute on every slider drag. SciPy's minimize_scalar(bounded=True) is used only as a development-time cross-check against this result, never in the live request path. OR-Tools was not used: there is no combinatorial or integer structure here that would justify it -- this is a 1-D problem.

Total(B) is additionally convex whenever the Punitive Demurrage rate is set at or above the normal Demurrage rate -- the intended configuration -- because demurrage is then a convex, non-decreasing function of Idle(t), which is itself convex in B. Exactness of the search does not rely on that: it needs only piecewise-linearity and a complete breakpoint set. Convexity is what guarantees the single-minimum shape the cost curve below displays.
