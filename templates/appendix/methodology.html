Why Breakpoint Search, Not a General Solver

Total(B) is a finite sum of max(linear, 0) hinge terms plus a linear term in B, so it is convex and piecewise-linear. A convex piecewise-linear function's minimum over a continuum is always attained at one of its breakpoints -- the sorted union of every day's Required(t) and Required(t) − SpotCap. Enumerating those breakpoints and evaluating cost at each is exact (not an approximation like a grid search), and fast: O(N) candidate points × O(N) vectorized cost evaluation for N ≈ 300–365 days -- well under the latency budget needed for the What-If Simulator to recompute on every slider drag. SciPy's minimize_scalar(bounded=True) is used only as a development-time cross-check against this result, never in the live request path. OR-Tools was not used: there is no combinatorial or integer structure here that would justify it -- this is a 1-D convex problem.

Breakpoints on the Cost Curve

Every marker is an exactly-evaluated candidate; the curve's minimum among them is the recommendation.
